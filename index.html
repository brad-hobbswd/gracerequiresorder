01

THE CENTRAL TRUTH

Grace should
change me.

Grace is one of the most beautiful expressions of the heart of Jesus, but grace was never given to become an excuse for irresponsibility.

I can extend grace to others, but I cannot pretend that extending grace excuses me from putting my own life in order. I cannot spend all of my energy understanding everybody else's failures while refusing to confront my own.

I cannot continually call myself selfless because I am willing to sacrifice for others while the responsibilities already placed in my own hands remain neglected.

“
If ye love me, keep my commandments.
John 14:15 — KJV
02

SELFLESSNESS & RESPONSIBILITY

Selflessness does not abandon responsibility.

The most loving thing I can sometimes do is handle what belongs to me before my failure becomes someone else's burden.

01

Handle What Is Mine

If something has been placed in my hands, I should not continually expect another person to carry it for me.

02

Consider the Other Person

Love asks how my choices affect someone else before my neglect becomes their burden.

03

Do Not Hide Behind Grace

Grace covers failure, but grace was never designed to become a hiding place where responsibility can disappear.

Sometimes the most selfless thing I can do is fulfill my responsibility so faithfully that another person does not have to rescue me, compensate for me, or continually extend grace for something I could have prevented.

03

GRACE & REPENTANCE

GRACE

“You failed, but I will not destroy you.”

Grace gives mercy where failure has occurred and creates room for restoration, healing, and another opportunity.

VS.
ENTITLEMENT

“I failed, so you should keep carrying it.”

Entitlement turns another person's kindness into permission to remain irresponsible and repeat the same pattern.

REPENTANCE DOES NOT SAY

“It is fine. Keep doing what you are doing.”

↓

REPENTANCE SAYS

“You have another opportunity. Now walk differently.”

04

PUT YOUR HOUSE IN ORDER

Before I reach outward, I must look inward.

Sometimes I want to give grace to everyone around me because it feels easier than dealing with what is happening inside of me. I can become so focused on understanding someone else's behavior that I never stop to ask why I am overwhelmed, why my responsibilities are unfinished, why my words are becoming careless, or why I am continually reaching a breaking point.

Eventually, disorder in one area begins affecting every other area. When I neglect what belongs to me, someone else may have to carry it. When I fail to keep my commitments, someone else may have to compensate for me. When I refuse to address my own behavior, someone else may have to endure the consequences.

01 My life must have order.
02 My heart must have correction.
03 My words must have restraint.
04 My commitments must have integrity.
05 My love must have action.
06 My grace must have truth.
05

THE EXAMPLE OF JESUS

He gave grace without abandoning obedience.

01

He Served

He humbled Himself and served others without abandoning His purpose.

02

He Forgave

He extended mercy without compromising righteousness or truth.

03

He Finished

He fulfilled what He came to do and remained faithful to His calling.

Jesus loves me exactly where I am, but His love is too powerful to leave me satisfied with remaining there.

06

THE DEVOTIONAL

I will still fail. I will still need mercy. I will still need forgiveness. I will still make mistakes. That is why grace is necessary. But needing grace should never become an excuse for refusing growth.

Jesus gives me grace when I fall, but He does not call me to live permanently on the ground. He forgives me, but He also calls me forward. He restores me, but He also calls me to walk differently.

I must stop asking how much grace I can receive while continuing to live this way and begin asking, because I have received so much grace, how faithfully can I now live?

Grace is not permission to remain out of order.

Grace is the mercy that gives me another opportunity to get back into order.

ONE QUESTION

Am I extending grace from a life that is being brought into order by Jesus, or am I using grace to excuse the responsibilities I already know I need to fulfill?

Sit with the question. Do not rush past it. Let the answer expose what needs correction, restoration, and obedience.
